How to Run a Successful Product Discovery Call

A product discovery call is not a feature wishlist collection session. If you enter a meeting asking a client, "What features do you want in this software?", they will ask for a complex dashboard, 12 report exports, and custom templates they will never use.

Instead, a product consultant should focus on uncovering workflows, pain points, and user goals.

Here is my playbook for discovery calls:

1. Focus on the 'As-Is' Workflow First

Ask the user to share their screen and walk you through their current daily tasks. Watch where they copy-paste, where they use Excel hacks, and where they wait for email replies. This is where the real requirements live.

2. The 'Five Whys' Elicitation Technique

When a stakeholder says, "We need a button that exports all data to PDF," ask why they need that PDF. You might discover they email it to a director who manually enters the summary into another app. The true requirement isn't a PDF export button; it's a backend system API integration.

3. Identify the Cost of Inaction

Always ask: "What happens if this process fails, or stays manual?" This helps you calculate the return on investment (ROI) and prioritize backlog features.

4. Co-Design High-Level Workflows Live

Use collaborative tools like Miro or Lucidchart to map flowcharts live. When stakeholders see their process visualized, they quickly spot gaps in their logic.
