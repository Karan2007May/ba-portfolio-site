The Practical Difference: BRD vs. FRD

In my role as a Business Analyst, the most common document point of confusion I see is the distinction between a Business Requirements Document (BRD) and a Functional Requirements Document (FRD). Many teams treat them as interchangeable, resulting in developers getting bog-down in business goals, or business clients getting lost in technical API specifications.

Here is the simple, practical breakdown:

1. The Business Requirements Document (BRD)

  • Target Audience: Project Sponsors, Business VPs, Product Managers.
  • Key Question: What is the business problem, and why are we building a solution?
  • Core Elements: Business goals, success metrics, scope boundaries, user personas, and high-level business rules.
  • Example: "The procurement system must route all purchases over $5,000 to the Division Vice President for cost control compliance."

2. The Functional Requirements Document (FRD)

  • Target Audience: Engineering Leads, QA Testers, System Architects.
  • Key Question: How will the system behave to solve that business problem?
  • Core Elements: System actions, data validation formulas, state transitions, database constraints, and API contracts.
  • Example: "When a purchase order's 'total_cost' field exceeds 5000.00, the system must query the Staff Directory DB for the active VP of the associated 'department_id', set the 'pending_approver_id' to that VP's ID, and trigger a mobile push notification."

By cleanly separating the Why/What (BRD) from the How (FRD), you ensure that business clients can easily review and sign off on target outcomes, while engineering squads get clear, actionable rules they can build immediately.
